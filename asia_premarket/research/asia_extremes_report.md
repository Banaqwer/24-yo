# What characterises the Asia high and the Asia low (CME NQ / ES, Globex 18:00-02:59 ET)

Scope: 579 Asia sessions per symbol, 2024-06-18 .. 2026-11-09, hourly yfinance bars in ET; 15-minute bars only for 2026-08-31 .. 2026-11-09 (48 sessions). Populations: the section-1 base rates and Table 2 use the 578/579-row population (NQ 2025-09-16 dropped; the 16 feed-truncated 6-8 bar holiday nights and the NQ 2025-12-16/17 splice nights are still in it); the hold-rate tables of section 4, the detector tables of section 5 and every number in the review of section 8 use the 560 clean full sessions per symbol (contract-splice and roll sessions excluded, 9 bars only). Section 8.1 item 6 lists how the base rates move between the two populations (every cell < 1.5 pt). ES has its own base-rate table in section 8.3A. Halves: H1 = before 2025-09-01, H2 = after. "Anecdote" = n < 30. Every statistic was reproduced by at least two independent refuters; where they corrected a lens the refuters' numbers are used. Knowability: **18:00** (before the reopen), **bar k** (at the close of hourly bar k) or **ex post**.

## 1. Answer in one page

**Timing is a U with a heavy left shoulder for the low.** Hour of the NQ high by bar k = 0..8: 23.5, 8.1, 11.1, 8.6, 5.2, 5.5, 5.5, 8.1, 24.4 %; of the low: 31.1, 8.6, 10.7, 7.4, 6.4, 3.5, 3.5, 8.6, 20.2 % (n = 579). Bar 0 (18:00-18:59) holds the low more often than the high: paired difference +7.6 pts, 90% CI [+2.9, +12.5], p = 0.012 (ES +5.7), independent of the RTH direction (p = 0.56). The bar-8 high-vs-low asymmetry (25.3 vs 20.8 %) is **not** established (CI [-0.2, +9.2], flips in 3/10 quarters). The interior of the U is the arcsine shape a driftless random walk produces, with two departures on the hourly population: a Tokyo-afternoon trough (k = 5-6 at 0.3-0.7x the null) and the first-hour low excess (1.4x; Sunday reopens 42 % vs 28 % weekdays). A Tokyo-open-hour bump in EDT sessions (k = 2 holds 12.7 % of highs vs 7.9 % under the null, n = 403) is regime-dependent, not a population fact: 8.4 % in H1 (the null) and 17.0 % in H2 (ES 9.4 -> 14.0 %), so it belongs with the 2026 survivors listed in section 7. The China/HK-open bar is never different from its neighbours (p >= 0.27 NQ, >= 0.5 ES; T12).

**Where they sit is almost deterministic from the 17:00 close; when they print is not.** Median hi_bp +27, lo_bp -23, range 62 bp. The Asia high exceeds the RTH high 31.8 % of the time, the low breaks the RTH low 25.1 %, both on only 0.4 % of nights (NQ 2/560; ES 1.3 %). P(high > RTH high) is the survival function of hi_bp at the 18:00 distance to the RTH high: 94.8 % when the 17:00 close is within 0.10 of a 20-session median range (n = 58, CI [0.90, 0.98]), 1.3 % beyond 1.5 ranges (n = 157, CI [0, 0.025]); the low side is the same ladder, slightly less steep (C1, C2, T10; 18:00). The extremes do **not** cluster at reference prices: no excess within +-5 bp of the RTH high/low/VWAP/open, prior Asia high/low, 17:00 close, previous settle or round numbers (16 levels x 2 symbols, spike ratios 0.44-1.44, every CI touching 1.0; T9).

**What precedes them at 18:00 is a volatility state and a side, not a clock.** VIX terciles set the median range at 44.7 / 60.4 / 83.2 bp (n ~ 193 each, monotone in 10/10 quarters; C4). After an up day the high exceeds the RTH high 48 % and the low breaks the RTH low 12 % (n = 307); after a down day 13 % and 40 % (n = 271) - the distance ladder restated (C3, F6). Forty continuous RTH-day features and 25 calendar flags shift P(extreme in the first or last bar) by at most 12 points at the chance hit rate (10/500 NQ, 14/500 ES at p < .05), and a walk-forward 35-feature model has negative skill on all ten timing targets (T4, C10, Detector B). Session direction is a coin flip under every 18:00 condition (continuation 40-50 %, every CI covering 47 %; F14).

**Intra-session, the extreme is read from distance, age and the bar's own shape.** At the close of bar k the close's position in the running range dominates: with the close in the bottom quarter the high is already in 80 % at k = 2, 94 % at k = 4-5, 97 % at k = 6 (n = 108-130 per k) versus 14-30 % in the top quarter; lows mirror it (T5). bars_since_hi >= 2 AND pos_in_range < 0.35 AND running range >= the 20-session median gives P(high in) = 0.974 (n = 382 bar-closes on 120 sessions, CI [0.961, 0.987]; H1/H2 0.975/0.973; ES 0.966, n = 385; an in-sample rule, 0.972 on the OOS window); an extreme set in the current bar holds 24 % whatever the range (T6). Through the 09:00 Europe open the same rule holds only 0.751 (ES 0.668): a bar-8 Asia extreme is exceeded in Europe 80-85 % of the time (section 8.3B). At the close of bar 0, a close in the top 20 % of its range puts the low in bar 0 58 % (n = 132) and the high 4 %; the bottom 20 % flips this to 45 % / 8 % (n = 92; T1). These are largely distance-to-extension facts a random walk reproduces (M4), which is why they hold in every quarter.

**Shared events.** Sunday reopens put the low early (by the Tokyo open 65.1 % vs 44.1 %, n = 106; the high is unaffected; fading to +10 pts in 2026). Monday nights put the low in bar 0 half as often (18.4 % vs 34.1 %, n = 114). FOMC eves make the low first almost always (high first 1/18 NQ, 2/19 ES; anecdote that survives leave-5-out and FDR). Mega-cap earnings and |16:00-17:00 bar| >= 25 bp nights are 1.4x larger with a 1.8x first bar but place the extremes no differently. A >= 3 % prior Kospi day adds ~ +10 pts to P(an extreme in the Tokyo-open bar) in the 2026 regime. Scheduled Asia-hours releases put the extreme in their bar 7.6 % vs 8.2 % expected (n = 380). Tier-1 data eves, BoJ, RBA, opex, month-end, quarter-end, holiday eves and the China open carry nothing (section 6).

**Lows are sharper than highs** (ex post): the low bar has 1.33x its hour's median range, 1.27x volume and closes in its upper half (0.68); the high bar is only mildly distinctive (1.09 / 1.10 / 0.45; n = 551 each, 10/10 quarters; T11). NQ and ES set the high in the same hourly bar 76.0 % and the low 81.4 % (n = 558; independence 16-18 %); neither leads (T13).

## 3. Events

**Early-low nights share two scheduled events.** Sunday: low by Tokyo open 65.1 % (n = 106) vs 44.1 %, identical in both halves (66.0 / 64.2 %), both DST regimes (EST tokyo_open_k = 1: 72.7 %, n = 33; EDT k = 2: 61.6 %, n = 73), independent of gap sign and Friday direction; ES 60.4 % vs 44.4 % (p = 0.0035). The visible mechanism is the reopen gap (median |gap| 18.0 vs 2.5 bp) and a 1.6x first bar, but weeknights with an equally wide first bar reach only 35 % / 48 % (n = 116), so it is not first-bar size alone. The edge is fading: +30 pts in 2024-25, +10 pts in 2026 (54.1 vs 43.9 %, n = 37, p = 0.28). FOMC eve (n = 18, anecdote): low before high 17/18 (ES 17/19), close above the 17:00 close 17/18, never in the bottom fifth of the range (0/37 combined); the "low in bar 0 56 % / high in bar 8 56 %" detail dies on leave-5 (p = 0.55 / 0.32).

**Late-high nights.** No 18:00 flag survives. TSMC results nights (14:00 Taiwan = bar 7/8) put the high in the last two bars 9/10 vs 0.33 base and 0.11 on neighbouring Wednesdays (n = 10, 8 dates inferred, 8/10 up-nights; candidate flag only; F13). This is ex post: the event calendar has no TSMC rows and the dates come from the catalogue narratives, so the 9/10 is not a forecastable flag until a verified results calendar exists.

**Tokyo-open-bar extremes.** |Kospi| >= 3 %: any extreme in bar tokyo_open_k 37.5 % (n = 88, CI [0.28, 0.46]) vs 22.8 %, but 78/88 nights are H2 and the all-night Tokyo-bar rate rose from 0.10-0.19 (2024-25) to 0.21-0.38 (2026), so the regime-adjusted lift is ~ +10 pts (Mantel-Haenszel OR 1.9, p = 0.020; ES 1.8, p = 0.036) and nil inside 2026Q2-Q3; low in the last bar 11.4 % vs 22.4 % (p = 0.003). The effect concentrates where |Kospi| and |SOX| are both >= 3 % (47 %, n = 34). A low set in the Tokyo bar is the *weakest*-aged candidate at later k (P(low in | k = 3/4/5) 0.53/0.59/0.67 vs 0.74/0.80/0.84 for a bar-0 low).

**China-open and event-time clustering: null.** Over 380 event-sessions (China CPI/PMI 09:30 CST, Caixin 09:45, activity 10:00, trade, Japan CPI, Tankan, RBA) the extreme falls in the event bar 29/380 = 7.6 % (CI [0.057, 0.102]) vs 8.2 % expected, in the event or next bar 14.2 % vs 14.8 % (ES 6.3 / 13.2 %), every half-year within 5 pts; BoJ 2/19 in the decision bars. The best cells (Caixin low in the Tokyo bar 20.4 % vs 9.1 %, p = 0.012, where ES shows the *high*) are what 108 tests produce. The "1.23x wider bar after China 09:30 data" rests on 5 nights; the Japan-CPI bar is quieter in volume only (0.77x NQ, 0.74x ES). Fewer last-bar extremes on China-data weeknights (36 vs 47 % NQ, 32 vs 46 % ES) is ES-only after leave-5 and fails FDR (E10).

**Size events, no timing effect.** Mega-cap earnings (n = 54): range 83.0 vs 60.5 bp (9/9 quarters), first bar 38.0 vs 21.3 (p = 1e-7), |gap| 12.3 vs 2.8; extremes in bar 0 27.8 / 33.3 % vs 22.9 / 30.7 % (p = 0.42 / 0.66). The 16:00-17:00 bar's sign says which RTH level is taken (55.6 % vs 31.5 %, p = 3e-4; ES 58.5 %), but a third of those nights already close beyond the level; with the level still ahead NQ shows 33 % vs 26 % (n = 36, p = 0.35), ES 48 % vs 25 % (n = 42, p = 0.002) - encode the signed distance (E7). FOMC day (n = 19): range 81 vs 61 bp (ES 62 vs 38); its timing numbers fail leave-5 and ES (F7).

**What did not matter:** tier-1 data eve (range 57 vs 62, p = 0.63; the "later low" is an H1 artefact once Sundays are removed from the base), tier-1 data day on weeknights (n = 40), BoJ (19), RBA (18), Japan data (38), ECB tomorrow (19), Fed minutes (18), roll (9), China policy (6), opex/witching (Sunday effects in disguise), month-end (10/24 are Sundays), quarter-end (n = 9, range = base), NFP-Friday Sundays (Fisher p = 0.044, 1 of ~2,600 tests, gone after 5 nights), yen moves (n = 192, no footprint), the "Kospi-open inflection" and the US-origin vs Asia-origin shock rule (continuation 48 % vs 47 %). The catalogue's big nights (n = 103, ex post) put an extreme in bar 0 64 % vs 37 % and in the Tokyo bar 20 % vs 36 %, but membership is selection on |asia_ret| (62/62 nights >= 60 bp are in it) and range-matched non-catalogue nights show the opposite shape; no 18:00 proxy recovers it (F1).

**Extreme-bar signature** (ex post; T11): low bar rng_rel 1.33 (CI [1.25, 1.38]), vol_rel 1.27 (k >= 1), close_loc 0.68, upper-quarter close 41 % vs 29 %; high bar 1.09 / 1.10 / 0.45; at k = 8 the extreme bar closes *at* the extreme. **Minute-level anatomy (48 sessions; every cell n < 30, anecdote).** Bar-0 extremes are made in the 18:00-18:14 slot 14/22 (NQ) and 14/25 (ES) times and coincide with the 18:00 open print 0/22 and 0/25 (upper bound ~10 %): a push inside the first 15 minutes, 0.3-18 bp from the print. Bar-8 lows fall in the 02:45 slot 8/16 (NQ) / 7/11 (ES), bar-8 highs 4/13; the bar-8 result vanishes on leave-5 and reverses in November. Middle-bar extremes are uniform over the four slots. The first-15-minute high/low holds 7/48 (14.6 %) = the arcsine null. NQ and ES set the extreme in the same quarter-hour ~50 % - a 2026Q4 crash-regime low against 76/81 % same hourly bar in the population. The window is a late-low regime (low in the last bar 33 % vs 19 %, low by Tokyo 29 % vs 50 %; p = 0.024 / 0.007) at 2026-level ranges; high-side rates match the population (M8).

## 7. Caveats and next steps

**Data.** The yfinance 18:00 hourly bar reports zero volume in 79 % of sessions (the 15-minute feed shows ~5,000 contracts), so volume is undefined at k = 0 and volume shares use 8 bars. Fourteen holiday-adjacent nights lack the 00:00-02:00 bars (CME traded to the 13:00 halt), so "short session" is a feed artefact and no range or timing claim about holiday eves is possible until the bars are sourced. The holiday_tomorrow flag never fires on true holiday eves (calendar_features skips the holiday) and disagrees with pre_holiday (9 vs 8 sessions, 2-3 overlap): re-code as one flag. The September-2025 roll leaves old-contract prints in NQ/ES 2025-09-17 and ES 2025-09-16 (95-108 bp wicks on 2-11k volume): now in the shared splice list (section 8.7); the verifier's wick scan lists 49 NQ / 19 ES candidate bars, mostly genuine high-vol nights. `build_sessions` double-maps two RTH dates (gap_days <= 3): require 1 / 3. usdjpy_chg may be stamped after 18:00 ET (no contamination detected). The lenses ran ~1,100-2,600 comparisons; the headline effects have p < 1e-5 and survive FDR, the calendar effects in the p = 0.01-0.05 band do not and are priors only.

**Regime drift.** 29 months with three shock windows (Apr-25 tariffs, Feb-Apr 2026 Iran war, Nov-26 crash). Regime-shaped survivors: the Sunday early low (+30 pts 2024-25, +10 pts 2026), the Tokyo-open-hour bump and the Kospi effect (2026 only), the hi_bp/lo_bp medians, the remaining-downside tail (8-15 bp deeper in H2), NQ/ES same-bar agreement (48 % in 2026Q4 vs 76 %). Expanding base rates handle part of this; a rolling-100 window adds < 0.002 Brier.

**15-minute history.** Ten weeks is an anecdote for everything sub-hourly. With the full history: P(session extreme | 18:15 close location) as a knowable feature; whether the 02:30-02:59 low is a trend into Europe; where inside the hour the EDT Tokyo-open excess sits; whether the first-15-minute level holds above the arcsine null; a cross-contract confirmation feature (agreement ~50 % at 15m in the window, 76-81 % hourly).

**Event timestamps.** BoJ rows lack time_et (use a DST-aware {3,4} EST / {4,5} EDT window); add tsmc_results_tonight (verify 8 of 10 dates); the China-data families map correctly to china_open_k and the Japan 18:30/19:30 releases to tokyo_open_k - 1, so the event-bar null is not a mapping error.

**Integration into the daily report.** At 17:xx print, per symbol: P(high > RTH high) and P(low < RTH low) from the scaled distance (the 7-bucket ladder or Detector B's raw p), the range P20/P50/P80 and hi_bp P80 from the vol state, the expanding hourly U with the Sunday/Monday/FOMC-eve prior shifts on the low side, and no direction. At each bar close print p_high_done / p_low_done (raw ensemble), the marginal P80 (what is knowable at bar k; the `_if_open` P80 conditions on an ex-post state and is printed beside it with that caveat), and the three human-readable flags: bar-0 close location (T1), the pos_in_range x k lookup (T5/F10) and the three-condition rule (T6). The review in section 8 found that, as first written, neither detector could run before the 23:00 bar closed and neither guarded against a partial bar; section 8.7 records what was changed after the review (live row builders, complete-bar guard, one splice list including the roll sessions, overnight targets, regenerated tables). Still open: drop or gate the isotonic step, report log-loss beside Brier, publish the (k x bars_since x pos) histogram as the baseline, set OMP_NUM_THREADS=1 and cache predict_now per date.

### 8.5 What breaks live (verified in `live_check.py`; items 1-3 and 5 were fixed after the review, see 8.7)
1. **Neither detector can run at 17:20 ET or at the close of bars 0-4.** `asia_extremes._asia_groups` (`len(grp) >= 6`) and `sessions.build_sessions` (`len(asia) < 6: continue`) both drop the night until the 23:00 bar closes (k=5), and with no 18:00 bar `asia_gdate` is NaT so `build_extremes` skips the RTH date. Tonight's files (2 bars of 2026-11-09 present) reproduce it: `predict_now('NQ','2026-11-09',0)` -> ValueError "no panel row", `predict_tonight('NQ','2026-11-09')` -> KeyError "not an RTH date". Truncating the hourly file at 18:00 / 20:00 / 22:00 of 2026-11-05 gives the same; at 23:00 the row appears. Fix: a `live_row(sym, date, bars_so_far)` that builds the 18:00 feature row from the sessions table without requiring an Asia session, and the bar-k panel row from the partial bars (the per-hour rolling medians and med20 come from history and are already ex ante).
2. **No complete-bar guard.** `data/NQ_1h.csv` as delivered (refreshed 19:35 ET on 2026-11-09 by its timestamp) ends with a 19:00 bar whose close 28059.00 is the 19:35 print (the 15-minute file has only the 19:00 and 19:15 bars, close 28063.50): an in-progress bar sits in the file as bar k=1. `predict_now` takes k from the caller and never checks that the last bar's timestamp + 1 h <= now. Add the check and drop the trailing partial bar before building the panel row.
3. **Splice lists differ.** `detector_preasia.SPLICE_SESSIONS["NQ"]` lacks 2025-12-16/17 although the NQ hourly file also toggles contracts there (18:00 bar 2025-12-16: O 25332 / L 25059 / volume 0; every bar swings 250-300 pts; the extremes row carries range 160 bp, lo_k=1, hi_k=8, and a 291-bp "RTH range" on 12-17). Those rows are in Detector B's training set and labels; align with `detector_intrasession.SPLICE_DATES` and add the roll sessions of section 7.
4. **Partial-session labels.** On a session with 6-8 bars the panel labels are relative to the bars present (last-bar high_done = 1.0 on all 16 truncated nights). Harmless for prediction, harmful if a refresh between 23:00 and 03:00 (like the 19:35 one above) ever writes the incomplete night into the tables that training reads; `n_bars == 9` protects the walk-forward but not a live `predict_now(..., full_sessions_only=False)` on a 13:00-halt night, whose k dummies assume 9 bars. Gate on the holiday calendar.
5. **DST on the switch weekend.** `is_dst` / `tokyo_open_k` come from the first Asia bar's timestamp; a live builder that took them from the RTH date would be wrong on the Friday before each switch (RTH 2026-10-30 is EDT, its Sunday reopen 2026-11-01 18:00 is EST, tokyo_open_k = 1). Derive them from the 18:00 timestamp of the night.
6. Minor: cross-asset `dchg` forward-fills exchange holidays, so `kospi_ret` / `n225_ret` repeat the previous value on 6.0-6.4% of rows (a Korean holiday makes `abs_kospi_pct` fire twice); `_oos_for` silently runs a full walk-forward (minutes) at 17:20 if the OOS CSV is missing; `_bundle` refits two classifiers and eight quantile GBMs per date (fine, but set OMP_NUM_THREADS=1 as section 7 says); `MAX_K = 7` so a k=8 call gets all-zero k dummies.

**Live row builders (8.5.1).** `asia_extremes.live_extremes_row(sym, date, h=None)` builds the 18:00-knowable row for a night that has not started or has not finished (`is_dst`, `tokyo_open_k`, `china_open_k` from the night's 18:00 timestamp, by calendar rule when no night bar exists yet), and `live_panel_row(sym, date, k=None, asof=None, h=None)` builds the bar-k row from the bars so far through the same per-bar feature function the stored panel uses.
