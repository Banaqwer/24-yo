# NQ/ES Asia-session (18:00–03:00 ET) regime narrative, 1 Feb – 30 Apr 2026

Companion to `catalogue_2026Q1_Apr.csv`. All index/oil/time figures below are taken from the cited pages (mostly CNBC live blogs, which timestamp overnight updates in ET). Conventions: "Asia session" = CME Globex 18:00 ET to 03:00 ET next morning. For Friday rows the Asia session is the following **Sunday 18:00 ET → Monday 03:00 ET** (Globex is closed Friday evening). Times are ET unless stated.

## A. Month-by-month regime narrative

### February 2026 — AI-disruption / software unwind, private-credit wobble, tariff ruling; Nasdaq −3.3%

**Dominant themes**
- **AI disruption of software, not AI capex, was the February story.** Software fell >20% YTD by month-end (Workday −24%, Gartner −25%; IGV ~30% below its high) on fears that agentic AI (Anthropic's Claude Cowork / Claude Code Security) commoditises core software; cybersecurity names dropped 5–9% on 20 Feb. ([YCharts Feb wrap](https://get.ycharts.com/resources/blog/monthly-market-wrap-february-2026/), [CNBC 20 Feb](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/02/19/stock-market-today-live-updates.html))
- **Macro data were delayed by the partial government shutdown** (ended 3 Feb): Jan NFP released Wed 11 Feb (+130k vs 55k, UE 4.3%, 2025 benchmark revised to +181k from +584k; indexes ~flat); Jan CPI released Fri 13 Feb (2.4% y/y vs 2.5%; core 2.5%) — futures flat, yields lower, June cut odds ~83%. ([CNN](https://www.cnn.com/2026/02/11/business/us-stocks-bonds-january-jobs-report), [CNBC CPI](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/02/13/cpi-inflation-report-january-2026.html))
- **Tariffs:** 20 Feb SCOTUS 6–3 ruled IEEPA does not authorise tariffs; Trump replaced them with a 10% Section 122 global tariff (effective the following Tuesday; a 15% rate was threatened). Equities rose that day (Nasdaq +0.9%). Q4 GDP 1.4% vs 2.5%.
- **Fed:** on hold at 3.50–3.75% since January; Kevin Warsh was formally nominated as Fed chair on 4 Mar (reported earlier); March cut odds <3% by end-Feb.
- **Private credit / bank stress:** Blue Owl halted retail fund withdrawals (19 Feb); UK lender MFS collapsed and GS/MS fell ~7% intraday on 27 Feb; later BlackRock-HPS (6 Mar), Morgan Stanley (12 Mar) and Apollo BDC (23 Mar) capped redemptions.
- **NVDA earnings (Wed 25 Feb AMC):** rev $68.1B vs $66.2B, Q1 guide $78B — stock +1–3% after hours, then **−5% on 26 Feb** (worst since Apr 2025), dragging AVGO/AMAT/LRCX 6–7%; the debate shifted to "sustainability of AI capex" and the stalled $100B OpenAI deal. Overnight NQ futures were flat at 18:00 (+0.02%). ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/02/26/nvidia-nvda-stock-price-q4-earnings.html))
- **27 Feb:** hot Jan PPI (core +0.8% vs 0.3%) gapped the market down pre-open; NQ recovered open-to-close (+0.51% RTH) despite NVDA −4% and bank stress. 10y dipped below 4%.
- **Oil was irrelevant until the last day:** Brent $71.90 at end-Feb; gold $5,400 (+8% in Feb), bitcoin −22% (5th monthly fall).
- Cross-asset: value > growth (IWD +2.6% vs IWF −3.4%); EM +5.5%, EAFE +4.6% — Asia outperformed the US in Feb.

**What Asia sessions typically did:** February overnight sessions were quiet relative to March; the only large overnight catalyst in the window was NVDA (flat at 18:00, selling came in RTH). The regime flipped on the night of **Sunday 1 March**.

### March 2026 — The Iran war oil shock; S&P −5.1% (worst month since 2022), Nasdaq −4.8%, Q1 Nasdaq −7%

**The shock (28 Feb – 4 Mar).** US–Israeli strikes on Iran began Sat 28 Feb ("Operation Epic Fury"), killing Supreme Leader Ali Khamenei. Iran retaliated on Israel, US bases and Gulf states and the IRGC closed the Strait of Hormuz (traffic −70% within hours; ships "set ablaze" threat 3 Mar). Sunday-night futures: NQ −1.5% to −1.7%, WTI +8–9%, gold +2.4%, DXY +0.65%, **yen weakened 0.56%** (net oil importer; safe-haven status failed), **Treasuries sold** (inflation fear beat haven bid). ([CNBC global wrap](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/03/02/global-markets-after-iran-strikes-oil-surges-airlines-sink-bonds-defy-safe-haven-playbook.html))

**How it propagated through Asia hours.**
- *Korea/semis were the transmission channel.* Kospi −7.2% (3 Mar), then >−12% intraday with circuit breakers (4 Mar; Samsung −7%, SK Hynix −5%; EWY −14%), −6% (9 Mar), −6.5% with halt (23 Mar), −5% intraday (30 Mar), −4.3% (31 Mar), −4.5% (2 Apr). On relief nights it was the upside channel too: +5% (10 Mar, 18 Mar), +8.4% (1 Apr), +7% (8 Apr). The Nikkei moved roughly half as much (−3.1% 3 Mar, −5.2% 9 Mar, −3.5% 23 Mar, −4.0% 30 Mar; +5.2% 1 Apr, +5.4% 8 Apr).
- *Oil during Asia hours was the signal.* WTI/Brent gapped on the Sunday reopen at 18:00 and set the tone for NQ: >$100 for the first time Sun 8 Mar (WTI +11.7%; Brent spiked to $119.46 at the Asia open on 9 Mar); Brent first close >$100 on 12 Mar; Dubai crude record $166 on 19 Mar; Brent $112 (20 Mar), $114 (27 Mar), $118.35 settle (31 Mar, highest since 2022); WTI record +35% week (2–6 Mar); March = largest monthly oil gain on record. IEA 400M-bbl + US SPR 172M-bbl releases (11 Mar) were judged insufficient — futures fell from −0.2% to −0.9% between 18:03 and 19:40 ET that night.
- *Yen:* behaved as an oil-importer currency, not a haven — one of the worst G10 performers over the conflict alongside SEK/CHF; leveraged funds rebuilt yen shorts by late April; USD/JPY ~159–160 by late April; verbal warnings (Katayama) and reported intervention on 30 Apr (yen +3%). KRW hit its weakest since 2009 (1,529.9 on 31 Mar). ([MUFG 28 Apr](https://www.mufgresearch.com/fx/fx-daily-snapshot-28-april-2026/))
- *Rates:* yields rose on inflation rather than falling on growth: 10y 4.18% (6 Mar) → 4.42% (24 Mar) after a weak 2y auction; CME FedWatch priced >50% odds of a **hike** by YE on 27 Mar, then 5.5% on 30 Mar after Powell's "good place / wait and see". The 2y dropped 21bp intraday on 23 Mar when Trump posted about "productive" talks.
- *Chips (SOX/SMH):* a second, independent leg down in late March — Micron peaked at a record close on 18 Mar (blow-out print, EPS $12.20 vs $9.31) and then fell six straight sessions (−22% by 26 Mar, −10% on 30 Mar, >−30% in 8 sessions); Google's TurboQuant compression algorithm hit memory names (26 Mar); SMH −10.6% in March (worst since Dec 2022); NVDA entered a bear market (>21% off 29 Oct high) on 30 Mar; SMCI −27% on an indictment (20 Mar). Then SMH +26% MTD by 28 Apr.
- *Technical levels broke in sequence:* 2026 lows for all three indexes 12 Mar; S&P 200-dma (6,590) lost 19 Mar; S&P below the Nov low 6,522 on quad-witching 20 Mar; Dow into correction 27 Mar; Nasdaq into correction 26 Mar (13% off record by 27 Mar); S&P cycle low **6,316.91 intraday 30 Mar**; VIX >30 on 9 Mar (35.3), 23 Mar, 27 Mar (31.05 close = peak) and 30 Mar.

**Policy during the shock.** FOMC 17–18 Mar held 3.50–3.75% (Miran dissent); median still one 2026 cut but 7 of 19 saw none; Powell "not as much progress as hoped"; stocks sold off on decision day (Dow −1.63%, first close below 200-dma since Jun 2025) and all 2026 cuts were priced out the next morning. BoJ 19 Mar held 0.75% (8–1, hawkish dissent for 1.0%) and flagged upside oil-driven price risk. Feb CPI (11 Mar) was in line and "stale"; Feb NFP (6 Mar) −92k (Kaiser strike) with UE 4.4%; PPI hot on 27 Feb and 18 Mar.

**How it ended (23 Mar – 8 Apr).** The first relief came **after** Asia hours on Mon 23 Mar (Trump "productive talks" post at 07:39 ET: futures +2.7%, WTI −10%). The diplomatic track (Pakistan mediation) then produced a sequence of overnight headline shocks in both directions: Trump "in negotiations" (24 Mar, Asia +); 15-point US plan rejected (25 Mar); IRGC closes Hormuz to US/allied shipping, hike odds >50% (27 Mar); Houthis join war, Trump "take the oil" (Sun 29 Mar); **WSJ: Trump willing to end hostilities even with Hormuz shut (Mon 30 Mar evening → oil reverses)**; Trump "leave in 2–3 weeks", Pezeshkian open to ending war (31 Mar RTH; Kospi +8.4%, Nikkei +5.2% overnight); Trump prime-time address "hit extremely hard for 2–3 weeks" (**21:00 ET 1 Apr → WTI $98→$109, NQ −0.8 to −1.3%, Nikkei −2.4%, Kospi −4.5%**); Iran–Oman monitoring protocol (2 Apr RTH reversal); "Power Plant Day" ultimatum (Sun 5 Apr 18:09); and finally **Trump's 18:51 ET post on Tue 7 Apr** suspending attacks for two weeks against Iran reopening Hormuz — the largest Asia move of the year: NQ +2.68% (range 3.46%), WTI −12% by 18:57 and −14.75% by 03:15, Nikkei +5.39%, Kospi +~7%, DAX +4.8%, VIX 31→20. The ceasefire took effect 8 Apr. ([CNBC 7–8 Apr](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/04/07/stock-market-today-live-updates.html))

**What Asia sessions typically did in March**
- *After US down days:* usually **continued lower when the catalyst was the war/oil** (2 Mar → Kospi −7%; 11 Mar → SPR disappointment; 18 Mar → Qatar LNG strike, Brent +11%; Sundays 8, 22, 29 Mar all opened −0.7% to −1.5%). But two of the five listed down-Friday rows (13 Mar, 27 Mar) printed **positive Sunday-night NQ (+0.98% each)** — in both cases oil reversed lower inside the Asia window (13/15 Mar: Trump's call for an international Hormuz escort coalition; 29 Mar: no single headline found — see Unknowns).
- *After US up days:* **faded when the up day was rhetoric-driven and oil had not fallen** (2 Mar RTH reversal → −1.31% overnight; 1 Apr → −1.51% on the speech) and **continued when hard de-escalation/oil declines were in train** (17 Mar +0.65% on chips/exports, 31 Mar +1.17%, 7 Apr +2.68%).
- The Asia range (1.1–3.5%) was persistently wide; in the listed rows range exceeded |return| by ~0.5–1.0pp, i.e. most nights had a two-way swing (typical path: gap at 18:00, extension or reversal around the 21:00–23:00 Tokyo/Seoul open, drift into 03:00 as Europe took over).

### April 2026 — Ceasefire, blockade, record highs; S&P +10.4% (best since Nov 2020), Nasdaq +15.3% (best since Apr 2020), Kospi +31% (best since 1998)

**Dominant themes**
- **Geopolitics turned from shock to two-way headline risk:** ceasefire (8 Apr) → violations claims (8–9 Apr) → Islamabad Talks fail, US naval blockade (Sun 12 Apr 18:20: NQ −1.2%, WTI +8%) → Trump "they'd like to make a deal very badly" (13 Apr RTH reversal, S&P highest close since pre-war) → Israel–Lebanon truce (16 Apr) → ceasefire extended indefinitely just after the close 21 Apr (NQ +0.4% at 18:01; Nikkei record; S&P/Nasdaq records 22 Apr) → stalled talks and Brent back to $112–120 in the last week. Brent never traded below ~$88–95 even during the ceasefire; Hormuz stayed effectively closed (21 transits vs ~130/day pre-war).
- **Inflation prints were energy-driven and looked through:** March CPI (10 Apr) 0.9% m/m, 3.3% y/y with core 0.2%/2.6% (below) — muted; UMich sentiment 47.6 record low, 1y expectations 4.8%; Feb core PCE 3.0% in line (9 Apr); ISM services prices 70.7 (6 Apr). March NFP (Good Friday 3 Apr, markets closed) +178k vs 59k.
- **Earnings were the upside engine:** >80–84% beat rate, blended growth ~27%; megacap AI capex guides went **up** (META $125–145B, GOOGL up to $190B) and the market punished the spenders (META −8.6%, MSFT −3.9% on 30 Apr) and rewarded the beneficiaries (GOOGL +10%, AMZN +4%); AVGO +6% on Google/Anthropic chip deals (7 Apr); AMZN +3% AH on $25B Anthropic investment (20 Apr); a WSJ report that OpenAI missed internal targets hit ORCL/AVGO/AMD on 28 Apr. Software kept lagging (IGV −27% YTD by 10 Apr). Nasdaq posted a 12-day winning streak (longest since 2009) into 16 Apr.
- **Policy:** FOMC 28–29 Apr held with an 8–4 vote (widest since 1992; Miran for a cut), "inflation is elevated" on energy; Powell's likely last meeting (Warsh succession). BoJ 28 Apr held 0.75% with a 6–3 split (widest under Ueda), raised FY26 core CPI to 2.8%, cut growth to 0.5%; USD/JPY slipped below 159; **reported MoF/BoJ yen-buying intervention 30 Apr (yen +3% at 10:16 ET)**. Japan PM Takaichi ordered 20 days of oil reserve releases (10 Apr).
- **Asia leadership:** Kospi +31% in April to a record 6,641 (chips/exports; KKR–Samsung SDS), Nikkei records on 16, 22 and 27 Apr (59,917), but both fell into month-end on BoJ/oil/Fed (Nikkei −1.02% 28 Apr, −1.06% 30 Apr).

**What Asia sessions typically did in April:** overnight moves became **headline-reversal prone rather than trend-following** — the blockade night (−1.2%) was fully reversed in the next RTH; the 21 Apr extension barely moved Asia (truce already seen as open-ended); after up days Asia generally continued modestly (13/14 Apr, 14/15 Apr) and after the megacap-earnings night (29 Apr) NQ futures were only +0.2% at 18:12 despite ±6–7% single-stock moves.

## B. Repeating patterns (testable statements)

1. **Sunday 18:00 ET gaps during the war were set by weekend oil/Iran headlines and were directionally right for the Asia window in 5 of 7 Sundays** (1 Mar −, 8 Mar −, 22 Mar −, 5 Apr −, 12 Apr −; exceptions 15 Mar and 29 Mar where NQ finished +0.98% after −0.3%/−0.7% opens). Test: sign(NQ 18:00–18:30 move) vs sign(18:00–03:00) on Sundays, conditional on |ΔWTI at reopen| > 3%.
2. **A WTI move of >±5% at or after the 18:00 ET reopen predicted the sign of the NQ Asia return in every identified case** (8 Mar, 9 Mar, 11 Mar, 18 Mar, 30 Mar, 1 Apr, 7 Apr, 12 Apr). Oil, not US futures, was the leading indicator in the first 60 minutes. Test: regress NQ(18:00–03:00) on WTI(18:00–19:00) over Mar–Apr; expect β<0 with R² far above the Feb baseline.
3. **Kospi was the highest-beta expression of the NQ overnight move** (|Kospi| ≈ 2–3× |NQ Asia| on war nights; Samsung/SK Hynix led both ways). Test: Kospi open (20:00 ET) vs NQ 18:00–20:00 move; expect slope >2.
4. **Trump posts/remarks between 18:00 and 21:30 ET were the single most frequent overnight catalyst** (8 Mar 18:13, 22 Mar weekend ultimatum, 29 Mar, 1 Apr 21:00 address, 5 Apr 18:09, 7 Apr 18:51, 12 Apr 18:20, 21 Apr ~16:05). Their market impact scaled with whether they changed oil supply expectations (Hormuz open/closed), not with tone. Test: tag overnight headlines as "Hormuz-relevant" vs "rhetoric"; compare absolute NQ response.
5. **RTH reversal days (open-to-close sign opposite the gap) that were not accompanied by an oil decline were faded overnight**: 2 Mar (RTH +1.63%, Asia −1.31%), 1 Apr (RTH +0.58% ahead of the speech, Asia −1.51%). Conversely RTH rallies with oil down ≥3% carried into Asia (31 Mar, 7 Apr, 13 Apr).
6. **Relief rallies began off-hours:** the three largest two-day NQ rallies of the window all started with an overnight or pre-market headline (23 Mar 07:39, 30 Mar WSJ evening report, 7 Apr 18:51), not in RTH. Test: share of cumulative Mar–Apr NQ gain accruing 16:00–09:30 vs 09:30–16:00.
7. **Scheduled US macro (CPI 11 Mar, 10 Apr; NFP 6 Mar, 3 Apr; FOMC 18 Mar, 29 Apr) did not generate large Asia moves by itself** — the overnight sessions around them were flat at 18:00 (±0.2%) unless an Iran/oil headline coincided. Only the 18 Mar FOMC had a lasting rates effect (cuts priced out the next morning). BoJ decisions (19 Mar, 28 Apr) moved USD/JPY <1% and did not register in NQ Asia ranges.
8. **Quad witching / roll:** CME roll day 12 Mar coincided with 2026 lows and a −0.9% evening slide on the SPR headline; 20 Mar quad witching produced the window's largest RTH loss (−1.56%) with selling accelerating on a 14:43 Reuters headline and then a −0.8% Sunday gap. Testable: realised RTH range on expiry/roll days vs non-event days during the same VIX regime.
9. **Chip-sector idiosyncratic shocks hit RTH, not Asia:** NVDA (26 Feb), MU (19 Mar), SMCI (20 Mar), TurboQuant (26 Mar), OpenAI-miss report (28 Apr) all landed pre-market/RTH; the corresponding 18:00 ET futures prints were within ±0.2%. Exception: Korean chip reactions (Samsung/SK Hynix) amplified NQ overnight on the H200-approval/exports night (17–18 Mar).
10. **Safe-haven playbook inverted:** on war-escalation nights Treasuries sold (yields up), the yen weakened, and gold often fell on the biggest risk-off days (−5% on 3 Mar and 23 Mar; −10% w/w by 19 Mar). Test: correlation of overnight NQ with USD/JPY should be positive (yen down = risk-off) rather than the usual negative carry relationship.
11. **Asia range ≫ |Asia return|:** in the ten listed rows the median range/|return| ratio ≈1.4; even the +2.68% ceasefire night had a 3.46% range (early +0.8%, then +~3% peak, fade on Iranian violation claims). Fading the first 18:00–19:00 impulse was wrong on headline nights but right on no-headline nights (24 Mar +0.9% at 18:02 → Nikkei +2.9%; 8 Apr flat → Asia −1%).

## C. Unknowns / could not verify

- **Sun 29 Mar – Mon 30 Mar (+0.98% NQ Asia after −0.7% open):** every retrieved source shows Asia cash plunging (Nikkei −3.97%, Kospi −5% intraday) and Brent rising to $115 during the window; Trump's "talks making progress" remarks are timestamped 07:48 ET (after 03:00). I could not find a headline inside 18:00–03:00 that explains a +0.98% close; candidates are quarter-end pension rebalancing demand (not verifiable without a dealer note — my search budget was exhausted before a Goldman/JPM rebalance estimate could be pulled) or a data/alignment issue in the supplied print. Treat as unverified.
- **Conflicting Asia closes for 31 Mar:** CNBC's US blog reports Nikkei −0.13%/Kospi −1.86% while CNBC's Asia wrap reports Nikkei −1.58%/Kospi −4.26% for the same session; the latter looks like the final close. Both are cited.
- **Nasdaq.com monthly reviews (March, April) returned HTTP 503 repeatedly**; month-end figures are therefore taken from CNBC's 31 Mar and 30 Apr closes instead.
- **CME roll-date page (503):** 12 Mar 2026 is inferred as the quarterly roll Thursday (expiry Fri 20 Mar) from CME's standard convention, not from the retrieved page.
- **Jan/Feb USD/JPY levels and the exact date Brent printed its $126 peak** were not pinned to a date (Wikipedia gives the level without a date; Dubai $166 on 19 Mar is dated).
- **NQ-specific Asia-hours timestamps for 15–16 Mar:** the oil reversal (WTI $100.37 → $98.70 by 20:10 ET, $93.50 Monday settle) and Trump's 15 Mar demand for a NATO/China escort effort are documented, but the WSJ escort-coalition report's publication time was not retrieved; attribution is inference from sequence.

- **Web search budget (200/turn) was exhausted after the second research pass**; remaining items were retrieved by direct URL fetch. Items not attempted as a result: a dedicated SOX/SMH daily series, dealer positioning/CTA notes, and Reuters wire pieces (Reuters pages generally block fetch).
